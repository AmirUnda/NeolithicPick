## Claims supported at site level

The institutional text describes:

- an extensive flint-mining area around Rijckholt and Sint-Geertruid;
- a thick Maastrichtian limestone deposit containing multiple flint layers;
- Neolithic vertical shafts approximately 6–16 metres deep and 1–1.5 metres
  in diameter;
- an estimated 5,000 shafts with horizontal passages;
- flint and deer-antler picks as mining tool classes;
- approximately 7,000 **flint picks** found at the Rijckholt mine;
- a mining peak around 3150 BCE, associated in the text with the Michelsberg
  culture;
- Rijckholt’s comparison with Spiennes, Grimes Graves, and Krzemionki.

These statements concern the mining landscape and tool classes. They are not
object-specific provenance for the digitised antler pick.

## Object-level assessment

The adjacent page contains an uncaptioned photograph of an antler object and a
flint object. The available photographs do not establish that the pictured
antler is the exact digitised object. No visible caption, inventory number,
collection name, acquisition record, findspot, excavation context, individual
date, or species identification accompanies it.

The photographs therefore do **not** resolve the viewer’s current unknowns:

The Centre Céramique author credit establishes responsibility for this
interpretive page, not ownership or custody of the object.

## Editorial consequence

The photographs reinforce the current cautious public wording. In particular,
the figure of approximately 7,000 refers to flint picks, so it must not be
restated as a count of antler picks. Existing fully identified geological,
mining-method, and radiocarbon sources remain the public citations for site
context.
